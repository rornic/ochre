# Landforms

How the ground gets its shapes beyond what tectonics and erosion make. Water is `docs/water.md`; climate and biomes are `docs/climate.md`. The one rule: landforms read the ground, never the live climate or biome output, so the water can be routed over the ground it will really stand on (`landform.rs`, `source.rs::landform_ground`). Valleys read a snapshot of warmth and moisture taken once before they are cut, as cold uplands already read warmth, and keep it in `map.valley`, so the block-level ground reads that stored layer and never the climate itself.

## The pipeline

`worldmap::generate`: tectonics, geology, hydrology, erosion, the floor, then in order fjords, cones, inselbergs, cold uplands, tower karst and cirques (`shapes.rs`), the landmarks (`meso/landmarks.rs`) and desert canyons (`meso/canyons.rs`), the mountain passes, the tableland terraces (`landform::terrace`), the valleys (`valleys.rs`), scatter, the last hydrology run, landform, climate, roughness and labels. Everything a shape or terrace does to `map.height` is therefore in place before the last water is routed.

## Map-scale shapes (`shapes.rs`)

Counts come from `LandformKnobs::from_seed`, not stored in the map, and the config weights them; no shape reads climate output.

- **Fjords:** a share of the mouths a mountainous coast has room for. A trough runs inland 40 to 120 cells, its floor 32 to 144 blocks under the sea, its walls rising 40 blocks a cell, ragged, so the sea fills it.
- **Cones:** a share of the sites volcanic ground has room for, 90 cells apart. Radius 16 to 36 cells, height 150 to 400 blocks, a crater a quarter as wide and a quarter of the height deep under the rim.
- **Inselbergs:** up to 8 rounded domes, 20 to 45 blocks high and 4 to 10 cells in radius, on dry and warm sedimentary lowland.
- **Cold uplands:** occasional flat-topped, steep-sided landmarks on cold lowland, 55 to 95 blocks high and 4 to 7 map cells in radius. Sites use regional warmth derived from config and height, before climate classification; their tops are broad enough for building.
- **Landmarks:** on flat ground between 20 and 700 blocks over the sea, in steppe, tundra, taiga, forest, grassland, desert, jungle, savanna and glacier (swamp has none: it is flat and wet), one landmark every 70 to 110 cells (a seed's draw). The biome is the climate's own classification of the cell's warmth at height and its moisture, before the climate pass. Each is a row of a rule table (`meso/landmarks.rs`), and the rock under the site picks which rows can form: a butte (130 to 260 blocks high, 2.5 to 4 cells in radius) needs a hard cap (sedimentary or volcanic ground), a ridge three times as long as wide (110 to 260 high) needs dipping beds (sedimentary or metamorphic; taiga's are moraines, on any rock), and a massif, a dome of 180 to 380 blocks over 6 to 11 cells, and a bowl, 40 to 120 deep, form on any rock. Weights and sizes differ by biome: desert favours buttes, grassland keeps to lower rolling hills and escarpments, forest and taiga to hills, ridges and basins that fill, jungle to steep hills, tepuis (a butte 180 to 320 blocks high over a cap) and sinkholes (a pit 1.2 to 2.2 cells across and 30 to 90 blocks deep with steep walls, in sedimentary ground, which keeps its lake like a bowl; forest and grassland have fewer), savanna to broad low domes, mesas and shallow pans, glacier to rock peaks and moraines. A seed favours some forms over others in each biome (0.3 to 1.9 times a row's weight, `favour`) stands its landmarks in a biome 0.75 to 1.3 times as tall and 0.85 to 1.25 as wide as the table (`scale`) and keeps 0.45 to 1.3 times as many of the sites it throws there (`crowding`), so one seed's desert is mostly buttes and another's domes, and no two feel the same. A butte stands with 1 to 4 smaller ones about it (1.6 to 4 radii off, 0.4 to 0.75 as wide, 0.45 to 0.85 as tall, `satellites`), as a worn-back mesa leaves a group, and a third of the massifs are drawn out into ranges 1.4 to 2 times as long as they are wide, and a ridge has a scarp, falling over about a third of the cells the gentle dip on its other side does. A bowl's basin is sited but not counted among the landmarks a ridge shows, and keeps its lake however small (`hydrology::run_keeping`; most hold one, a few dry out). The flattening leaves landmarks alone, and the passes are carved round them. Sized from `docs/meso-slice1-decision.md`. They keep about 95% or more of the height laid through the valleys; an alpine valley through a nearby range can still take a little.
- **Wadis:** 0 to 8 a seed (`LandformKnobs::wadis`), the dry beds of desert, savanna and steppe ground 30 or more blocks over the sea, 60 cells apart: a canyon's line (the water's), but 18 to 40 blocks deep, 1.4 to 2.4 cells to a side, all in the map's trench (no slot, no forks). They run dry unless rain gathers upstream. A trench of any of these is shielded like a landmark, so steppe's flattening and the valleys leave it alone.
- **Canyon style:** a seed's canyons and gorges cut 0.7 to 1.3 times as deep as below and run 0.8 to 1.5 times as wide (`LandformKnobs::canyon_depth`, `canyon_width`; the width also scales wadis, not their depth).
- **Canyons and gorges:** 0 to 4 canyons a seed (`LandformKnobs::canyons`) and 0 to 3 gorges (`gorges`), a canyon headed on desert plateau and a gorge, the same cut with rain on it, on the plateaus of savanna, grassland, forest, jungle, taiga and steppe, each headed on plateau at least 80 blocks over the sea and 150 cells from the next. A canyon follows the line the water will take (the priority flood's `down` pointers over the filled ground), so it always runs to the sea or the map's edge and drains. It has two parts. The map holds 60% of the depth, 54 to 120 blocks (less on low plateau, and never more than half the ground's height over the sea), as a trench 1.2 to 2.2 cells to a side swelling and pinching between 0.65 and 1.35 of that, with a flat middle and a steep wall, its floor falling at least 0.15 blocks a cell. The rest is a slot cut at block level along the line, which `WorldMap::canyon` marks cell to cell (the way to the next cell in the low nibble, the slot's depth in tens of blocks, 0 to 150, in the high one; `WorldTerrain::canyon_at`): 5 to 28 blocks across (a noise over 150 blocks), the line bent by up to 14 blocks, with sheer sides, never cut under the sea and not seen by cells 16 blocks or wider. Dunes are held off within 13 to 48 blocks of the line, so the floor is flat. From its deeper reaches (over 25 blocks) forks climb upstream to either side every 35 to 75 cells, 70% of the time: 25 to 70 cells long, 50 to 80% as deep and 60 to 85% as wide, their floors rising 1.5 blocks a cell from the main floor until they meet the ground, so they join it and drain into it. They cut through whatever stands in their way, a landmark's skirt included (a notch left a sill, and the trench behind it filled into a chain of lakes). Most run dry, as a desert wash does: a river forms in them only where enough rain collects upstream.
- **Eskers:** 0 to 6 a seed (`LandformKnobs::eskers`), each headed on taiga or tundra ground 15 or more blocks over the sea, 120 cells from the next. A ridge 0.8 to 1.4 cells to a side and 14 to 30 blocks high (swelling and thinning along it) is raised along the line the water will take (the priority flood's `down` pointers), 30 to 130 cells long, rising and falling over its first and last 10 cells. Landmarks stand untouched. The water the last hydrology run routes then finds its way beside the ridge.
- **Sea stacks:** 0 to 60 a seed (`LandformKnobs::stacks`), each a pillar 20 to 55 blocks over the sea and 0.7 to 1.3 cells in radius, steep sided, standing in water 3 to 28 blocks deep that lies 2 to 4 cells off land at least 25 blocks over the sea, 3 cells or more from the next (`meso/stacks.rs`).
- **Ponds:** 0 to 1500 a seed (`LandformKnobs::ponds`), shallow round pools 1 to 2.2 cells across and 4 to 10 blocks deep, with a flat floor, at least 4 cells apart on flat tundra and taiga ground 8 or more blocks over the sea (`meso/ponds.rs`). Their middles join the landmark bowls' mask, so hydrology keeps a lake in them however small: on seeds 2 and 3 the small lakes (30 cells or fewer) in cold country went from 39 and 12 to 544 and 177.
- **Tower karst:** up to 4 fields of 10 to 40 steep towers, 30 to 70 blocks high, on wet sedimentary lowland.
- **Cirques:** up to 6 bowls 40 to 100 blocks deep with a headwall, cut into cold mountains on the cold half of the config's temperature gradient.

## Base relief of jungle, savanna and glacier (`meso/relief.rs`)

The tectonics left jungle nearly flat (mean gradient 0.14 to 0.25 on seeds 1, 7 and 8, against 0.24 to 0.31 for forest). After the landmarks, each of the three gets layers of noise added to `map.height` in proportion to how much of its surroundings, softened over 5 cells each way, is that biome by the climate's own class, none within 5 blocks of the sea (full at 40) and none on a landmark: jungle a ridged noise 1100 blocks across and 42 high cut into ledges 9 blocks high (flat treads and steep risers, `stepped`), a second ridged noise 650 across and 16 high in ledges of 6, and a smooth swell 3000 across and 14 high, so the hills step round each other and are hard to cross, savanna a smooth swell 2200 across and 38 high with a ridged one 1300 across and 26 high between and a step 4200 across and 36 high, eased over a fifth of the noise's range (a plateau and a plain with an escarpment between, as Africa's old planation surfaces), glacier ridged ice 1800 across and 45 high over a swell 4500 across and 30 high. A seed makes each layer 0.6 to 1.5 times as tall and 0.75 to 1.4 as long. Jungle's mean gradient went to 0.25 to 0.39 (the share of it steeper than 0.2 from 16 to 48% before up to 42 to 57%, with 7 to 20% steeper than 0.6) and savanna's from 0.11 to 0.18 up to 0.17 to 0.23 (steeper than 0.2: 22 to 30%, 3 to 12% of it steeper than 0.6). Valleys keep off the steeper ground, so seed 1's steppe has fewer valley walls than it did.

## Rolling ground

The roughness layer (`detail::roughness_layer`) takes a seed's own extra roughness for the open ground of each biome but steppe and desert, 0 to 0.22 of its scale (about 0 to 8 blocks more detail, `rolling_by_biome`), so one seed's grassland is billiard flat and another's rolls.

## Tableland and terraces

Sedimentary ground 40 to 360 blocks over the sea that is not mountain is tableland in patches covering about the seed's `tableland_share` (`landform::tableland`). Its height is stepped into the rock beds (`WorldMap::terraced`: hard beds as benches with faces, clay as slopes) at the map level before water is routed, and again at block level, which sharpens the bicubic ramp across a face into a cliff. A river over tableland runs in a gorge: a wide flat floor and a steep wall.

## Cliffs

Ground steeper than gradient 1 is cut and built up into cliffs by 3D noise (changing 0.7 as fast with height round the ground as along it, since at full speed it turns the surface over several times in a column and leaves stray blocks), up to 9 blocks, up to 18 where the seed's `cliff_boost` reaches (mountain and tableland). There are no undercuts or overhangs cut into the mass beyond what that noise leaves (see `terrain-perf.md`).

## Relief features (`ochre-base/src/features.rs`)

Each is a pure function of the seed and position returning blocks to add to the height, at most 18, zero once a sample cell is 16 blocks wide, and laid by `WorldTerrain` after containment only where no water is near:

| Biome | Features |
| --- | --- |
| Glacier | crevasses (2 to 5 wide, 8 to 18 deep, with bridges, in the same fixed frame), seracs |
| Tundra | pingos, patterned ground, occasional broad low hills (10 to 18 high, 44 to 130 across, hundreds of blocks apart, in provinces 1600 blocks across of which two in five hold none) |
| Taiga | drumlins along the config's gradient angle |
| Desert | yardangs along the wind, swales across its slopes (gullies), and dunes (spacing, height, how far the rows bend, 0.3 to 1.8 dunes, and the gentle windward share of each, 0.5 to 0.78, are each a seed's own) |
| Savanna | kopjes (rounded rock heaps 6 to 14 high, bare stone where the heap stands 4 or more blocks over the plain, with grass round the foot), and swales across its slopes (gullies) |
| Steppe | none of its own: its shape is the valleys below (the gullies it had went unseen on its flat ground, and the kind remains for the foothills) |
| Swamp | hummocks |
| Grassland | knolls (low rounded hills 5 to 11 high and 45 to 100 across, in drifts) |
| Forest | dells (shallow hollows 3 to 7 deep and 28 to 56 across) |
| Jungle | mogotes (small steep limestone hills 10 to 18 high and 16 to 32 across, in clusters), and ledges (ridged noise 90 blocks across and up to 13 high cut into ledges of 3.5 that need a scramble, one 28 across and 4.5 high in ledges of 2, and one 9 across roughening every tread, so no stretch of the floor is smooth but the sections are long) |

`worldview <seed> features <x> <z> <half>` counts them.

## Valleys

Long troughs with flat floors, broad slopes and cliff bands, cut into inland ground (6 blocks or more over the sea, under about 220) after the tableland terraces and before the last water routing, so rivers find the floors (`valleys.rs`). A valley is a pure function of the seed and a place: a noise field along the world's ridge angle, bent at three scales so it meanders, with distance to its line measured as the field's value over its slope, so floors stay about 200 blocks wide where the field is flat as well as where it is steep. Floors are flat, slopes rise over about 200 blocks (gradient 0.2 to 0.35), and the cut is 40 to 70 blocks, deepening and shallowing along a valley's length (never under 40% of its depth).

Before the valleys are cut, steppe ground is flattened (`valleys::flatten`): pulled 90% of the way toward a copy of itself smoothed over 60 cells each way (about 4 km), averaged over land only, in proportion to how steppe-like the ground is (the same warmth and moisture band as the siting, softened over 40 cells so the zone has no cliff at its edge) and how far over the sea it is (none under 20 blocks, full from 80, so there is no coastal step). Mountains and the ground within 8 cells of one are left alone. It makes steppe broad open plain with long sightlines, removing hills smaller than a few kilometres. A bigger radius makes steep edges, and hills wider than the radius, such as foothills, remain: it flattens the ground and does not change where steppe grows.

About a third of a slope is cliff, in stretches a few hundred blocks long: a face 14 to 64 blocks wide that sits at a height up the slope which varies, with a ragged edge, so a wall is a broad slope in some stretches and a broken face in others.

`valleys::run` writes `map.valley`, the share of the cut each cell takes, 0..1 as a byte, sited from the ground as it stands before the cut: full on warmth 0.26 to 0.58 (after the height lapse) and moisture under 0.3, which is where `classify` puts steppe, with a quarter of it everywhere else on flat ground, scaled by the seed's `valley_share` (0.5 to 1) and by how far the ground is over the sea. Steppe is favoured, not required: a valley can lie in any biome. Two things keep valleys off water. None is cut within about 7 cells of a lake or of a basin hydrology will fill (a priority flood on the ground after the terraces finds them), and none within about 14 cells of a big basin (2,000 cells, 6 blocks deep), since a valley's sump at the flat rim of a big dry basin fills the whole basin into a lake that floods the plain round it. After the cut, the basins the valleys made (cells no basin held before) are drained by a channel cut through their dam, two rounds of the drain `hydrology::run` uses for small basins (`hydrology::drain_where`).

The cut is `depth * soft_mask * share`, so every LOD sees the trough. Block-level ground adds `depth * share * cliff * (soft_mask - sharp_mask)` (`WorldTerrain::valley_sharpening`), so the face steepens into a cliff while floor and plateau stay where the map put them. It is calmed near water like the tableland's beds, and fades out in cells wider than 16 blocks (none from 32). `TerrainSource::valley_wall` is 0..1 where a cliff face is: it extends `cliff_boost` and adds a gradient of 1 at full wall to the one blocks are chosen by (`terrain.rs::block_gradient`), so mid-steep faces show rock. LOD cells take the same bias so colours do not change at a swap.

worldview's `valley` probe and worldview's `hillshade` layer show them. `generate_with(seed, config, false)` makes the same world without valleys, to compare.

## Not built

Gneiss and andesite, objects (erratics, termite mounds), and LOD fidelity for any of this: see `world-plan.md`.

## Placed objects: boulders, giants and termite mounds

`generate/rocks.rs` sets single objects by a hash of position on grids of cells, one at most in each, and builds them block by block with the chunk, after the trees (a trunk keeps its place). A boulder is a half-sunk, squashed ball of the rock the geology has at its foot (`rock_column` at the ground it sits on, so marble on marble country, and not a stone of its own) with a nibbled rim, on ground up to gradient 0.9 and within 3 blocks of height under it, sunk to the low side. The taiga has them 2 to 5 blocks in radius in 4% of its 24-block cells. The tundra has three sizes and nothing in between, each scaled smoothly over fields 96 blocks across by a factor from 0.1 to 2 (`field`), so they come in drifts with open ground between. Small rocks, 0.9 to 2.3 blocks in radius leaning to the smallest, on a 12-block grid (`SMALL_*`, up to 30% of cells). Outcrops, elongated low ledges 5.5 to 11 blocks in radius and a third as tall as wide (`OUTCROP_*`), on the 24-block grid in about a tenth of its cells, on ground that may vary 6 blocks under them. Giants (`GIANT_*`), 5.4% of the cells of a 96-block grid, kept off the cell's edge: four fifths a lone tall boulder 7 to 11 blocks in radius, and one fifth an arch (`arch`), so an arch is a rare find, about one in 80 cells: two pillars 6 to 9 blocks in radius with a gap of 3.5 to 4.5 blocks between, to walk through, and a long thin capstone laid over the gap and resting on both, its underside 4.5 blocks over the ground. A giant's feet must be dry and within 8 blocks of height of each other, which with its pillars is why `ROCK_REACH` is 20 (the ledges alone need 12). The desert reuses the tundra's three sizes at lower shares: small rocks at 0.4 of the tundra's, ledges (`share` 0.6) in drifts, and giants at twice its rate, of which two in five are hoodoos (`hoodoo_cluster`): three to seven thick pillars 4.5 to 6.5 blocks in radius standing about 14 to 26 blocks over the ground, each under a cap 1.3 times as wide and 0.4 as thick resting on its top, scattered within 10 blocks, on ground that may vary 12 blocks under them. Other biomes borrow them at small shares: boulders in forest (2% of 24-block cells), grassland (3%) and glacier (30%), small rocks in taiga (0.3 of the tundra's rate) and, at a tenth of it, grassland and forest, and giants (lone stones and the odd arch) at half the tundra's rate in taiga, grassland, steppe and savanna and 0.3 in forest. A seed puts 0.4 to 1.8 times as much rock in each biome (`flavour`), so one seed's taiga is strewn with boulders and another's nearly bare. A termite mound is a cone of dirt 3 to 7 blocks high and 2.5 to 4 in radius on savanna (7.5% of cells), on level ground (gradient 0.4, within 1 block). None is placed on a mountain or volcanic modifier, within 3 blocks of water or under 3 blocks over the sea. In the cold their blocks are weathered at meshing time, dimmed to a plain darker grey (`docs/climate.md`, `GrassTint::weathered_at`), so marble boulders aren't bright white; placement and shape are untouched. In LOD meshes each is drawn as cells while its cell is small enough to show it: small rocks up to 2 blocks across, the rest on the 24-block grid up to 4, giants up to 8 (`ROCK_MAX_CELL`), each cell a rock if its middle is, past which one is under a pixel.
