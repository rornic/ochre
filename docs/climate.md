# Climate and biomes

How a cell gets a biome and what grows on it. Landforms are `docs/landforms.md` and read the ground alone; climate reads them, never the other way round.

## The config

A seed draws `warmth`, `humidity`, a temperature `gradient` and its angle, `continentality`, the wind direction and the plate count (`worldmap/character.rs`). `OCHRE_CHARACTER=off` keeps the config as given, and the tools (`worldview`, `bench`, the game) all build the world with the drawn character.

## Warmth and moisture (`climate.rs`)

Regional warmth is the baseline plus a warped gradient plus continentality over the distance from the sea; it falls with height at `LAPSE_PER_BLOCK`. Moisture comes off the sea along the wind, drops on rising ground and leaves rain shadows; humidity scales the rain. Both are blurred, rivers and lakes wet and cool their banks (not in cold country), and a Whittaker-style table (`classify`) gives the climate biome: glacier, tundra, taiga, grassland, forest, steppe, savanna, desert, jungle, with swamp on flat, low, wet ground by fresh water. The treeline moves with regional warmth.

## Biomes (`ochre-base/src/biome`)

A biome sets how thick trees grow, how big they get (`TreeScale`: height, spread, squash, a share of emergent giants), how lush or dry the grass reads (`climate_shift`), the blocks it uses, and its relief features. Mountain and volcanic are a `Modifier` on the climate biome, not biomes of their own, so a desert mountain and a taiga mountain differ by what is under them. A tenth of jungle trees are emergent giants on trunks 2.3 times the canopy's, up to 42 blocks, and the jungle's trees keep 90% of their density in a clearing (`TreeScale::clearings`, 40% elsewhere), so its canopy is nearly unbroken.

## Edges between biomes

The map holds, for each land cell, `biome_alt` (the climate biome across its nearest class boundary) and `biome_margin` (how far off the boundary it is, 255 well inside: the climate offset that changes the class, but never past two cells from a different class, since a slowly changing climate would otherwise scatter patches for hundreds of blocks). At block scale `biome_at` looks up the cell through a wander of 1.5 cells and a slow drift of up to 160 blocks, which bends edges over hundreds of blocks, and near a boundary a block takes the alt biome in patches of 10 to 20 blocks, thickest on the boundary. Cells 16 blocks wide or more use their own biome, so LODs do not change. `worldview <seed> edges` measures it.

## Trees and flora

Tree density is the map's per-cell density times a grove multiplier from noise at wavelength 160 (0.4 in a clearing to 1 in a grove, filtered for the sample's cell so wide cells see an average). Under trees, ferns and shaded grass are twice as thick in jungle and half as thick in taiga as in forest. Flora thins out in the cold, to none below temperature 0.10 (`COLD` in `flora.rs`).

Trees stay `Log` and `Leaves` voxels. A trunk is drawn as an eight-sided prism per log, a quarter of a block across on the smallest tree to a whole block on the tallest (`Tree::trunk_radius`), bowed up to 0.5 block along its tree's seeded lean, and any other log (a branch) as prisms reaching toward each log beside it, diagonals included. Trunks are one block thick in the voxels. A broadleaf has three to five lobes and its crown stands off its trunk by up to a fifth of its spread, any way, from its seed; a trunk leans further on colder, drier ground (`tree_in_cell` passes the climate's temperature and humidity as the tree's exposure) and each tree's leaf cards are 0.9 to 1.1 times the size of the next's. Savanna trees are umbrellas, a flat plate of leaves (squash 0.3) over a bare trunk whose branches run flat under it, jungle canopy trees are slender (a crown a third narrower than a broadleaf's on a trunk of 16 to 24 blocks), and a jungle giant is an umbrella of up to 9.6 blocks' spread (`TreeKind::Umbrella`, `Slender`, `scaled_tree`). A pine puts out four drooping boughs at the foot of each tier, as logs inside its crown, and branches are drawn at 40% of the trunk's radius. Leaves are drawn as painted cards, one on each leaf that touches the air, and the leaves inside a crown draw nothing but stay solid, so they still stop the player and the light. Leaves cannot be pointed at or broken. Designed in `docs/designs/trees.md`.

The jungle's ferns are a fifth big ferns (`BlockType::BigFern`, the fern sprite drawn 1.9 to 3.3 blocks tall in the shrub's three crossed pairs, 2.2 to 3.6 across) and the savanna's open grass is half again as thick as other grass, 55% of it long grass (`BlockType::LongGrass`, the tall grass sprite 1.7 to 2.7 blocks tall in clumps of two or three, tinted to straw) and a fifth short. Neither has a sprite of its own: they reuse the fern's and the tall grass's. The savanna has no poppies or dandelions, as the tundra has none.

Shrubs and heath (`flora_at`) grow in the open ground of some biomes, in clusters of about five, one at most in each 16 block square, a carpet of plants whose planes overlap: a cluster is a disc 2 to 3 blocks in radius filled at 50%, and a share of the squares hold one (`shrubs` in `flora.rs`): shrubs in grassland (35% of squares), savanna, steppe, swamp and the jungle (60%, and in its shade as well as out of it, with ferns in its clearings too, so no ground of it is bare), and heath, low and a deep reddish brown, in taiga (25%) and tundra. Tundra heath makes irregular carpets of radius 3 to 9 blocks, grouped in broad drifts with open ground between. None grow in shade (the jungle's apart), forest or desert. The tundra has no poppies or dandelions, and its long grasses go that colour to bridge between the heath: tall and seed grass tinted 70% toward `OXBLOOD_TINT`, short grass 28%, by how cold it is, full below temperature 0.08 and none above 0.17 (`GrassTint`), so a taiga has a little and a forest none. The cold's grass is a dull khaki, not the olive the dry cold palette gives (its red is only 1.05 to 1.24 times its green at the tundra's median, and about 1.0 at the warmest, wettest edge, where it read green): ground and tufts are pulled toward `BROWN_TINT` (0.46, 0.32, 0.17, about 0.63 saturation where the orange it replaced had 0.78) by a ramp that is full below temperature 0.12 and gone by 0.19 (`GrassTint::cold_at`), so the tundra's red is at least 1.3 times its green (1.2 at its warmest edge), and a taiga's colder half is toned down while its warmer half keeps its green. The cold's ground grass also has patches, all by the same cold factor and all smooth noise: reddish ones over 40 block cells, fully red past a threshold, about a third of the ground, tinted toward `RUSSET_TINT` (`GrassTint::russet_at`), and over them three kinds that make the ground a mosaic (`GrassTint::mosaic`, `STRAW`, `LICHEN`, `PEAT`): bleached straw, grey-green lichen and dark peat, covering 21%, 19% and 14% of the ground, from noise that mixes a field 150 blocks across, which says where each kind lies, with one 40 across that breaks it up (`MOSAIC_LARGE`, `MOSAIC_SMALL`). The russet goes on last. Peat stays lighter than `OXBLOOD_TINT`, so the heath, which is untinted and dark, still reads against it. The tufts of tall, seed and short grass take the same patches so a tuft is the colour of the grass it stands in. Seed grass in the tundra (the brown ramp, so not a taiga's edge) goes cream in wetter-looking patches, by noise over 18 block cells that peat adds to, about a fifth of the tundra's ground (only the seed grass in it is cream), so it reads as cotton grass among the grass (`GrassTint::cotton`, `COTTON_*`), with no texture of its own. Cold grass faces don't merge into larger quads, or a patch would smear across one, and meshes of cells wider than a block, the far land, keep the plain tint, since a third of it red read as a flat brown band along the horizon.

Rocks in the cold are weathered by the mesher, not by block type (`GrassTint::weathered_at`): every face of marble, limestone, granite, shale, basalt and the placeholder rock is multiplied by a dim grey scaled by the cold factor (marble 0.62, limestone 0.72, granite, shale and rock 0.8, basalt 0.9). This is every such block in cold country, cliffs and giants and arches as well as the small rocks, since the mesher can't tell them apart. The tint is one plain colour with no stains or patches, so the faces still merge into larger quads. Far terrain is dimmed the same. The painted rock layers multiply by the vertex colour (`world.wesl`), so it needs no shader change.

The camera's grade cools in the cold: `Surroundings::warmth` (smoothstep over temperature 0.15 to 0.35) eases the grade by `cold_color_grading` (white balance -0.02, overall saturation x0.94, shadows' saturation x0.92 at full cold; Bevy's grade has no per-range hue, so the cooling is the global balance) over about 2 seconds (`player.rs:apply_cold_grade`). It is laid over the camera's grade after `update_underwater_effects` and taken off before it (`lift_cold_grade`, which puts back the grade it saved), so the underwater grade and the dry defaults it snapshots never carry it, there is no drift, and in warm country the grade is untouched, bit for bit.

The tundra is bare ground, not snow: the climate would snow every block of it, but the biome thaws the snow below 350 blocks over the sea into tan grass, with gravel scree on ground steeper than gradient 0.38 (`Plain::with_thaw`), and the snow stays on the heights. Its grass is read from a drier, colder climate, and the cold dry end of the grass palette is tan. Taiga also thaws below 250 blocks above sea level, leaving grass or gravel on slopes at gradient 0.6 and above; higher ground keeps its snow.

Frost is a skin the land's shader (`frost.wesl`) lays over up-facing ground that the cold would freeze but the generator left bare, with no vertices and no blocks, so it works on any surface and `GENERATOR_VERSION` doesn't move. It follows the snow blocks' own rule (`ochre_base::cold`): the ground cools by one unit of temperature for each 2600 blocks of height over the sea, snow settles where it reaches `SNOW_TEMPERATURE` (0.1346), and frost runs from full at that temperature to nothing `FROST_BAND` (0.12) warmer, so it thins out toward the snow instead of ending in a line. The temperature is the camera's sea-level one (`TerrainSource::temperature`, read about four times a second and eased over two seconds in `frost.rs`) with the height taken from the fragment, so it is exact near the camera and wrong by the temperature's drift over about 100 blocks beyond a biome border. A biome scales it by `Biome::frost`, the camera's biome only: the tundra keeps 0.5 and the taiga 0.6 of what the cold gives, as they thaw what the climate would snow. The shader skips Snow, Ice and leaf tiles, ground facing under about 55 degrees of up, and ground under cover (baked sky light under about 0.3, which LOD and edited blocks carry too). Frost is not a flat tint. Patches are noise at 29 and 7 blocks, but up close each is judged plate by plate: Worley cells 0.38 blocks across are in or out as a whole by the noise at their middle and a bar of their own, so a patch's edge is angular and jagged. Plates differ in thickness, thin ones let the ground's colour and light and dark come through, and a faint seam of shade runs between them. A thin bright rim sits just inside each edge, and frost never covers more than 82%. Plates and small patches fade out as a pixel nears a fifth of a block, leaving soft big patches far away. Plants (flora tiles) are rimed from the tip down, using the sprite's own v, thick on their crowns. The glitter is round points, one in each cell of a 0.25 block grid on the surface, each with a random facet tilt that flashes when the sun's half-vector lines up with it, so it moves as the camera does, within a few blocks only, and each point swells and fades on its own clock (a cubed sine, 0.4 to 1.2 radians a second) and is never fully dark. A sheen lies over the plates: each leans a little its own way, so the sun's lobe breaks across them, and the sky shows on them at grazing angles. Plants have none. `RendererSettings::frost` turns it off, `DebugSettings::no_frost` compiles it out of the shader for timing.

The aurora (`aurora.wesl`, added to the night sky by `celestial()` in `stars.wesl` and, at 0.4 of the strength, to what water reflects) is curtains of green light with a violet upper fringe and a cyan foot in the northern (-z) sky, brighter the colder the ground under the camera is: nothing at or above the snow line's temperature plus a frost band, full a frost band below `SNOW_TEMPERATURE`, and none with frost off. The sky's pipelines have no curvature buffer to read the cold from, so `reflection` in `cloud_target.wesl`, which has, writes that sum (`aurora_chill`) into the cloud reflection map's third texel and `cloud_cover.wesl` reads it back there. It fades in with `night_factor()` and sits behind the clouds as the stars do, so cover and rain hide it. Nothing in it travels: time is a noise axis, so features brighten, fray and fade where they are, with a slow warp and a drift under 0.1 degrees a second; each stretch of a curtain lengthens and retracts on its own slow clock, and the top and foot fade out raggedly rather than at a line.

## Geography colour and heat

Grass is tinted by `TerrainSource::tint_climate`, the climate shifted by the lie of the land: banks wetter by `water_closeness` (a byte layer, 255 on any river, lake or pond and gone 3 cells out, `worldmap/water_closeness.rs`), crests drier and hollows richer by `exposure`, and high ground thinner of cover (`BANK_WETTING`, `EXPOSURE_DRYING`, `ELEVATION_THINNING` in `worldmap/source.rs`). The shift is in humidity, so the same push moves a savanna and a tundra along their own palettes. `GrassTint` samples it on a grid across a coarse LOD node, every 16 blocks and 17 a side at most, where a chunk keeps its four corners, so a node samples where the chunks beside it do and a river corridor shows without a pop at the hand-over. Leaves and flora take the same tint. Tree density reads the same attribute: `TerrainSource::vigour` is the humidity shift, and `tree_lie` turns it into a multiplier on a tree cell's chance, 1 plus twice the shift, from 0.2 to 2.2 times, so banks and hollows grow more trees and crests and high ground fewer. `tree_bound` takes the larger of it and 1 so LOD nodes still never skip a cell the full-detail trees would grow. Steep ground under vegetation goes to a dry earth in the fragment shader (`STEEP_*` in `world.wesl`), only on the side of a blend that carries the tint, so rock and scree keep their own colour. `drift` stays on top.

Banks and hollows green grass up only to `LUSH_CAP` (0.6 humidity, `worldmap/source.rs`), under the palette's lush end, which is the jungle's own.

Each kind of country has a feel (`atmosphere.rs`): `Feel` weights cold, hot, jungle, forest and steppe, 0 to 1, smooth in the climate (`Feel::of`), so they overlap where kinds meet and a taiga is a forest's weight with the cold's. A row of knobs per kind (`COLD`, `HOT`, `JUNGLE`, `FOREST`, `STEPPE`) says what each does at full weight, and `Atmosphere::of` sums them into the sun's strength and colour, the grade's white balance, saturation and midtone contrast, the haze's tint and density, and how deep a storm dims the light, exactly neutral at no feel.

- Hot (savanna, desert): a hard, bright, warm sun, a warmer, richer, harder grade, a thick warm haze.
- Jungle: hot, sticky, wet, vibrant: the most colour, a filtered sun, a thick, green, short haze (`JUNGLE.haze_density` 15 times, since the fog's range is tens of kilometres, taken at the fourth power of the jungle's weight so the edge of a jungle isn't hazed) that closes in on the trees past about a kilometre.
- Forest: sheltered, cosy, gloomier: a dimmer, warmer sun and a soft close haze. Taiga is this under the cold.
- Steppe: vast, desolate, exposed: drained colour, a hard clear light, thin haze so views run far, and storms dim the light half as deep again.
- Mountain (`TerrainSource::mountain_cover`, smooth across its edge): crisp, thin air, a clear hard light, far views, bluer shadows, and weather that comes up faster.
- Volcanic (a biome's modifier, a step at its edge that easing smooths for the camera): a dim red sun through a grey-brown ash haze, drained colour, maroon shadows.
- Cold: the grade is its own (`tonemap::cold_color_grading`, unchanged, so the tundra's look is as it was), and its row adds a bluer, denser haze, so cold country far off shows in the distance.

`Surroundings::feel` is sensed at the camera and eased over 2 s (`EasedFeel`). The grade (`view.rs`), the sun (`darken_sky` in `weather.rs`) and the camera's haze follow it. Far country shows in the haze: `ambience/haze.rs` senses the feel on 16 compass points at 1, 3 and 8 km and gives the shader each point's tint and density in the curvature buffer, and `fogged` multiplies the haze by them. The haze's edge against the sky and clouds is not tinted, so a seam can show. The shadows take their country's colour too: the camera's feel sets what painterly lighting pushes the sky's light toward in shadow, and how far (`shadow` in the curvature buffer, `painterly.wesl`), warm over hot country, green in the jungle, cooler and plainer on the steppe, with the cold's left as it was. This is camera-local only, so far country's shadows are not its own.

## Steppe

Steppe is the dry, mid-warm biome (warmth 0.26 to 0.58, moisture under 0.3) and is meant to read as vast, open and exposed. Its ground is made of three things, each described where it is made. The ground is flattened into broad plain and cut with valleys (`docs/landforms.md`, `valleys.rs`). Its cover follows how far each place stands over the ground round it (`exposure`, `worldmap/exposure.rs`: the height over the land average within 8 cells, 0.5 on level ground, up on crests, down in hollows, one byte per map cell read by `TerrainSource::exposure`). And its wind and sound are steadier than the grassland's.

Cover (`flora_at`, `steppe_thickness`): grass is thin and short on crests and thick, tall and seeding in hollows and in broad patches 180 blocks across, with variation inside them over 40 blocks. The share of ground with a tuft runs from 5% to 42%, short grass from 80% of tufts on a crest to 25% in a thick patch, and seed grass from 3% to 15%. The steppe's own plant is feather grass (`BlockType::FeatherGrass`, `feather_grass.png`): thin stems with silky plumes that fall like hair, pale (it is tinted by the climate like the other grasses, from a brighter greyscale), 6% of tufts on a crest and over half in a thick patch, and in no other biome. It was drawn with `foliage.py:feather_grass` from the `grass_top` painting, not by SDXL. Shrub clusters are about a quarter of grassland's (8% of 16-block squares) and shelter in hollows (0.4 to 1.6 times that), and its flowers are its own, a muted dusty-lilac one (`BlockType::SteppeFlower`, drawn by `foliage.py:steppe_flower`) in a few small drifts, thicker in hollows, about a seventh of grassland's: no poppies or dandelions, whose red and yellow read as lush meadow (the real steppe does have flowers, mostly small and subdued, irises, alliums, pasqueflowers and milkvetch among them). Bare earth shows in patches 60 blocks across on the most exposed crests only, up to about an eighth of the ground and none on level ground or in a hollow (`steppe_bare`, a `Dirt` top block in `chunk.rs`, and no plant on it). Trees stand only where exposure is under 0.38, in hollows (`tree_in_cell`), so they are few and sheltered. Outcrops, the tundra's wide low ledges, stand one in about 80 of the 24-block cells, more on crests and on valley walls (`rock_in_cell`).

Wind: the gust direction in the foliage shader is the world's prevailing wind and not a fixed one, and a second, slower gust band about 190 blocks apart rolls along it, over every biome's leaves and flora (`foliage.wesl`, `ChunkMaterial::wind`). The sound follows the shader's gust: `gust_at` in `ambience/wind.rs` is the same function as `gust_at` in `foliage.wesl` (keep the two in step), read at the camera every frame on the clock the shader uses, and it swells the wind's roar and whistle, so the sound rises as the grass bends. On steppe the wind has a floor that depends on how exposed the ground under the camera is (`Surroundings::exposure`): none in a hollow, where it comes and goes as elsewhere, a fifth of 0.5 on level ground and the full 0.5 on a crest (`STEPPE_EXPOSED`), over where it stops dropping out between spells. A hiss above 2.4 kHz follows the gusts for grass rustling. The shader's sway is not scaled by exposure, so grass in a hollow still bends while the wind sound is quiet there.

Ground texture: the grass ground of steppe draws its own layer, `steppe_top`, a rougher and less leafy one (earthy mottling, grit, a trace of the grass layer's leaves, and sparse pebbles), grey at grass's mean so the same tint colours it, with the pebbles coloured: rust-brown and pale, worked out against the steppe's golden tint (about 0.77, 0.58, 0.14, which leaves almost no blue), so under it they show as stones and not as lighter grass. The mesher picks it per face (`Tile::SteppeTop`, `TerrainSource::steppe`, `BlockSource::steppe`), since the shaders do not see the biome, and the steppe's edge is a hard change of texture, under a smooth tint, along one block's edge. Grass block sides on steppe draw it too. Far LODs (which draw the grass layer's mean, close in brightness) and steppe on a mountain or volcano keep the grass layer. `.claude/skills/block-textures/steppe_ground.py` draws it from `dirt.png` and `grass_top.png`, no ComfyUI.

Not done: steppe has no tint of its own beyond the biome's dry shift. A `GrassTint` ramp by exposure would need the grass faces not to merge, which costs vertices (`docs/terrain-perf.md`), and was left.

## Birds by biome

The starling flock and the goose skeins, which used to be the same everywhere, are sized by the biome under the camera (`birds.rs`: `BirdLife`, `bird_life`, read twice a second; crossing a border, the flock shrinks or grows toward the new size three birds a quarter second, so it does not pop). Grassland and the base terrain keep the old values. The rest of the sky:

| Biome | Starlings | Flock range, height | Skein every (s) | Geese, height |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| grassland | 160 | 55, 34 | 45 to 110 | 7 to 11, 48 |
| steppe | 60 | 85, 42 | 25 to 60 | 9 to 13, 70 |
| tundra | 25 | 70, 32 | 30 to 75 | 9 to 13, 45 |
| swamp | 110 | 55, 30 | 28 to 70 | 8 to 12, 40 |
| savanna | 110 | 75, 40 | 110 to 260 | 7 to 11, 55 |
| taiga | 50 | 50, 36 | 50 to 130 | 7 to 11, 55 |
| forest | 70 | 45, 38 | 90 to 220 | 7 to 11, 60 |
| jungle | 40 | 40, 40 | 200 to 420 | 6 to 9, 65 |
| desert | 20 | 85, 45 | 300 to 600 | 5 to 8, 80 |
| glacier | none | | 400 to 800 | 5 to 7, 80 |

Open and wet country has wide skies and busy geese (migrants crossing the steppe and tundra, fowl over the swamp); woods, jungle, desert and ice have few. Birdsong is still only the forest loop, so steppe and the other treeless biomes have none: that needs a recording per biome (`docs/issues.md`).
