# Climate and biomes

How a cell gets a biome and what grows on it. Landforms are `docs/landforms.md` and read the ground alone; climate reads them, never the other way round.

## The config

A seed draws `warmth`, `humidity`, a temperature `gradient` and its angle, `continentality`, the wind direction and the plate count (`worldmap/character.rs`). `OCHRE_CHARACTER=off` keeps the config as given, and the tools (`worldview`, `worldprobe`, `bench`, the game) all build the world with the drawn character.

## Warmth and moisture (`climate.rs`)

Regional warmth is the baseline plus a warped gradient plus continentality over the distance from the sea; it falls with height at `LAPSE_PER_BLOCK`. Moisture comes off the sea along the wind, drops on rising ground and leaves rain shadows; humidity scales the rain. Both are blurred, rivers and lakes wet and cool their banks (not in cold country), and a Whittaker-style table (`classify`) gives the climate biome: glacier, tundra, taiga, grassland, forest, steppe, savanna, desert, jungle, with swamp on flat, low, wet ground by fresh water. The treeline moves with regional warmth.

## Biomes (`ochre-base/src/biome`)

A biome sets how thick trees grow, how big they get (`TreeScale`: height, spread, squash, a share of emergent giants), how lush or dry the grass reads (`climate_shift`), the blocks it uses, and its relief features. Mountain and volcanic are a `Modifier` on the climate biome, not biomes of their own, so a desert mountain and a taiga mountain differ by what is under them. A tenth of jungle trees are emergent giants on trunks 2.3 times the canopy's, up to 42 blocks, and the jungle's trees keep 90% of their density in a clearing (`TreeScale::clearings`, 40% elsewhere), so its canopy is nearly unbroken.

## Edges between biomes

The map holds, for each land cell, `biome_alt` (the climate biome across its nearest class boundary) and `biome_margin` (how far off the boundary it is, 255 well inside: the climate offset that changes the class, but never past two cells from a different class, since a slowly changing climate would otherwise scatter patches for hundreds of blocks). At block scale `biome_at` looks up the cell through a wander of 1.5 cells and a slow drift of up to 160 blocks, which bends edges over hundreds of blocks, and near a boundary a block takes the alt biome in patches of 10 to 20 blocks, thickest on the boundary. Cells 16 blocks wide or more use their own biome, so LODs do not change. `worldprobe edges` measures it.

## Trees and flora

Tree density is the map's per-cell density times a grove multiplier from noise at wavelength 160 (0.4 in a clearing to 1 in a grove, filtered for the sample's cell so wide cells see an average). Under trees, ferns and shaded grass are twice as thick in jungle and half as thick in taiga as in forest. Flora thins out in the cold, to none below temperature 0.10 (`COLD` in `flora.rs`).

Trees stay `Log` and `Leaves` voxels. A trunk is drawn as an eight-sided prism per log, a quarter of a block across on the smallest tree to a whole block on the tallest (`Tree::trunk_radius`), bowed up to 0.5 block along its tree's seeded lean, and any other log (a branch) as prisms reaching toward each log beside it, diagonals included. Trunks are one block thick in the voxels. A broadleaf has three to five lobes and its crown stands off its trunk by up to a fifth of its spread, any way, from its seed; a trunk leans further on colder, drier ground (`tree_in_cell` passes the climate's temperature and humidity as the tree's exposure) and each tree's leaf cards are 0.9 to 1.1 times the size of the next's. Savanna trees are umbrellas, a flat plate of leaves (squash 0.3) over a bare trunk whose branches run flat under it, jungle canopy trees are slender (a crown a third narrower than a broadleaf's on a trunk of 16 to 24 blocks), and a jungle giant is an umbrella of up to 9.6 blocks' spread (`TreeKind::Umbrella`, `Slender`, `scaled_tree`). A pine puts out four drooping boughs at the foot of each tier, as logs inside its crown, and branches are drawn at 40% of the trunk's radius. Leaves are drawn as painted cards, one on each leaf that touches the air, and the leaves inside a crown draw nothing but stay solid, so they still stop the player and the light. Leaves cannot be pointed at or broken. Designed in `docs/designs/trees.md`.

Shrubs and heath (`flora_at`) grow in the open ground of some biomes, in clusters of about five, one at most in each 16 block square, a carpet of plants whose planes overlap: a cluster is a disc 2 to 3 blocks in radius filled at 50%, and a share of the squares hold one (`shrubs` in `flora.rs`): shrubs in grassland (35% of squares), savanna, steppe and swamp, and heath, low and a deep reddish brown, in taiga (25%) and tundra. Tundra heath makes irregular carpets of radius 3 to 9 blocks, grouped in broad drifts with open ground between. None grow in shade, forest, jungle or desert. The tundra has no poppies or dandelions, and its long grasses go that colour to bridge between the heath: tall and seed grass tinted 70% toward `OXBLOOD_TINT`, short grass 28%, by how cold it is, full below temperature 0.08 and none above 0.17 (`GrassTint`), so a taiga has a little and a forest none. The cold's grass is a dull khaki, not the olive the dry cold palette gives (its red is only 1.05 to 1.24 times its green at the tundra's median, and about 1.0 at the warmest, wettest edge, where it read green): ground and tufts are pulled toward `BROWN_TINT` (0.46, 0.32, 0.17, about 0.63 saturation where the orange it replaced had 0.78) by a ramp that is full below temperature 0.12 and gone by 0.19 (`GrassTint::cold_at`), so the tundra's red is at least 1.3 times its green (1.2 at its warmest edge), and a taiga's colder half is toned down while its warmer half keeps its green. The cold's ground grass also has patches, all by the same cold factor and all smooth noise: reddish ones over 40 block cells, fully red past a threshold, about a third of the ground, tinted toward `RUSSET_TINT` (`GrassTint::russet_at`), and over them three kinds that make the ground a mosaic (`GrassTint::mosaic`, `STRAW`, `LICHEN`, `PEAT`): bleached straw, grey-green lichen and dark peat, covering 21%, 19% and 14% of the ground, from noise that mixes a field 150 blocks across, which says where each kind lies, with one 40 across that breaks it up (`MOSAIC_LARGE`, `MOSAIC_SMALL`). The russet goes on last. Peat stays lighter than `OXBLOOD_TINT`, so the heath, which is untinted and dark, still reads against it. The tufts of tall, seed and short grass take the same patches so a tuft is the colour of the grass it stands in. Seed grass in the tundra (the brown ramp, so not a taiga's edge) goes cream in wetter-looking patches, by noise over 18 block cells that peat adds to, about a fifth of the tundra's ground (only the seed grass in it is cream), so it reads as cotton grass among the grass (`GrassTint::cotton`, `COTTON_*`), with no texture of its own. Cold grass faces don't merge into larger quads, or a patch would smear across one, and meshes of cells wider than a block, the far land, keep the plain tint, since a third of it red read as a flat brown band along the horizon.

Rocks in the cold are weathered by the mesher, not by block type (`GrassTint::weathered_at`): every face of marble, limestone, granite, shale, basalt and the placeholder rock is multiplied by a dim grey scaled by the cold factor (marble 0.62, limestone 0.72, granite, shale and rock 0.8, basalt 0.9). This is every such block in cold country, cliffs and giants and arches as well as the small rocks, since the mesher can't tell them apart. The tint is one plain colour with no stains or patches, so the faces still merge into larger quads. Far terrain is dimmed the same. The painted rock layers multiply by the vertex colour (`world.wesl`), so it needs no shader change.

The camera's grade cools in the cold: `Surroundings::warmth` (smoothstep over temperature 0.15 to 0.35) eases the grade by `cold_color_grading` (white balance -0.02, overall saturation x0.94, shadows' saturation x0.92 at full cold; Bevy's grade has no per-range hue, so the cooling is the global balance) over about 2 seconds (`player.rs:apply_cold_grade`). It is laid over the camera's grade after `update_underwater_effects` and taken off before it (`lift_cold_grade`, which puts back the grade it saved), so the underwater grade and the dry defaults it snapshots never carry it, there is no drift, and in warm country the grade is untouched, bit for bit.

The tundra is bare ground, not snow: the climate would snow every block of it, but the biome thaws the snow below 350 blocks over the sea into tan grass, with gravel scree on ground steeper than gradient 0.38 (`Plain::with_thaw`), and the snow stays on the heights. Its grass is read from a drier, colder climate, and the cold dry end of the grass palette is tan. Taiga also thaws below 250 blocks above sea level, leaving grass or gravel on slopes at gradient 0.6 and above; higher ground keeps its snow.

Frost is a skin the land's shader (`frost.wesl`) lays over up-facing ground that the cold would freeze but the generator left bare, with no vertices and no blocks, so it works on any surface and `GENERATOR_VERSION` doesn't move. It follows the snow blocks' own rule (`ochre_base::cold`): the ground cools by one unit of temperature for each 2600 blocks of height over the sea, snow settles where it reaches `SNOW_TEMPERATURE` (0.1346), and frost runs from full at that temperature to nothing `FROST_BAND` (0.12) warmer, so it thins out toward the snow instead of ending in a line. The temperature is the camera's sea-level one (`TerrainSource::temperature`, read about four times a second and eased over two seconds in `frost.rs`) with the height taken from the fragment, so it is exact near the camera and wrong by the temperature's drift over about 100 blocks beyond a biome border. A biome scales it by `Biome::frost`, the camera's biome only: the tundra keeps 0.5 and the taiga 0.6 of what the cold gives, as they thaw what the climate would snow. The shader skips Snow, Ice and leaf tiles, ground facing under about 55 degrees of up, and ground under cover (baked sky light under about 0.3, which LOD and edited blocks carry too). Frost is not a flat tint. Patches are noise at 29 and 7 blocks, but up close each is judged plate by plate: Worley cells 0.38 blocks across are in or out as a whole by the noise at their middle and a bar of their own, so a patch's edge is angular and jagged. Plates differ in thickness, thin ones let the ground's colour and light and dark come through, and a faint seam of shade runs between them. A thin bright rim sits just inside each edge, and frost never covers more than 82%. Plates and small patches fade out as a pixel nears a fifth of a block, leaving soft big patches far away. Plants (flora tiles) are rimed from the tip down, using the sprite's own v, thick on their crowns. The glitter is round points, one in each cell of a 0.25 block grid on the surface, each with a random facet tilt that flashes when the sun's half-vector lines up with it, so it moves as the camera does, within a few blocks only, and each point swells and fades on its own clock (a cubed sine, 0.4 to 1.2 radians a second) and is never fully dark. A sheen lies over the plates: each leans a little its own way, so the sun's lobe breaks across them, and the sky shows on them at grazing angles. Plants have none. `RendererSettings::frost` turns it off, `DebugSettings::no_frost` compiles it out of the shader for timing.

The aurora (`aurora.wesl`, added to the night sky by `celestial()` in `stars.wesl` and, at 0.4 of the strength, to what water reflects) is curtains of green light with a violet upper fringe and a cyan foot in the northern (-z) sky, brighter the colder the ground under the camera is: nothing at or above the snow line's temperature plus a frost band, full a frost band below `SNOW_TEMPERATURE`, and none with frost off. The sky's pipelines have no curvature buffer to read the cold from, so `reflection` in `cloud_target.wesl`, which has, writes that sum (`aurora_chill`) into the cloud reflection map's third texel and `cloud_cover.wesl` reads it back there. It fades in with `night_factor()` and sits behind the clouds as the stars do, so cover and rain hide it. Nothing in it travels: time is a noise axis, so features brighten, fray and fade where they are, with a slow warp and a drift under 0.1 degrees a second; each stretch of a curtain lengthens and retracts on its own slow clock, and the top and foot fade out raggedly rather than at a line.
